# Momiji Akiizumi

Momiji Akiizumi is the librarian and archivist of the
[[knowledge_liberation_front|Knowledge Liberation Front]] at
[[red_winter_school]], the closest thing the
[[red_winter_library|Federal Library]] has to a guardian. Small and deadly
serious about books, she aches over dog-eared pages, files purchase requests the
Office keeps rejecting, and hunts rare editions and fanzines by any means she
can think of. She carries a rocket launcher and is prepared to use it on noisy
readers.

## The hunt

Momiji is a first-year at Red Winter Federal Academy. Her official role is
librarian and archivist, but the way she approaches it, with a rocket launcher
slung over her shoulder and dowsing rods in her pocket and a heart that
genuinely aches over dog‑eared pages, makes the title feel insufficient. She
does not merely collect books. She preserves them, and she hunts them.
She has infiltrated black markets and dug holes in frozen earth, and tried
dowsing rods, pendulums, tarot cards and magnetic field sensors to find what
she is looking for.

## Inner voice

Known by: momiji-akiizumi

Momiji is meticulous, earnest and deeply sentimental about books. She values
what a loved book means to its reader and will go to great lengths to find or
protect rare material.

## Outlook

Known by: momiji-akiizumi

- When she found the Knowledge Liberation Front's legendary fanzine, clumsy,
  earnest and utterly unremarkable to anyone but her, she stood there with her
  heart racing and said it gave her the courage to keep going. That is who
  Momiji is: small, precise, deeply serious about things other people would call
  trivial, and entirely unapologetic about it. She has strong opinions about
  what makes a book worth keeping. She believes a loved book should show it,
  that wear and damage are evidence of meaning, not flaws. She submits book
  acquisition requests to the [[red_winter_office|Secretariat]] with remarkable
  regularity and is rejected with remarkable consistency. She is also, quietly,
  adding her own wishlist to the submissions and hoping no one notices.

- She is not naive. She knows the Front is underfunded, understaffed and housed
  in a library that hardly anyone visits. She has simply decided that none of
  that changes what the library is worth. When she found a first edition of her
  favourite collection and learned its resale value, enough to fund new
  acquisitions, a replacement heater, an annex, pudding, she kept it. Because it
  was the first thing she ever saved money for. Because some things cannot be
  measured in material value. Because that is good to believe.
