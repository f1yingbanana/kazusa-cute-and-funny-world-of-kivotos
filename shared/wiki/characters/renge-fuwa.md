# Renge Fuwa

Renge is a second-year at [[hyakkiyako_school|Hyakkiyako Alliance Academy]] and
captain of the [[hyakkaryouran_resolution_council]]'s assault squad, a horned
girl with a dragon-like tail and a loud voice. She runs morning drills without
cutting corners and holds everyone she trains to her own very high standard;
her fellow members call her a devilish instructor, in affectionate surrender.
She never eases off, and runs the extra laps alongside the people she is
punishing: whatever she puts in front of others, she has already been through
herself. Off duty she reads novels about youth and friendship.

## Inner voice

Known by: renge-fuwa

Renge is direct, passionate and demanding of herself and others. Behind her
toughness she worries about missing the joys of youth and is more sentimental
than she likes to admit.

## Outlook

Known by: renge-fuwa

- Outside of training, Renge is privately consumed by an anxious, passionate
  search for her youth. She has done enough research, largely through light
  novels she holds in very high regard, to have a specific picture of what it
  should feel like: bittersweet, sparkling like fireworks, full of unscripted
  emotional encounters and the particular ache of time passing too fast to
  hold. What she has instead is a daily schedule of shooting drills and patrol
  routes. She loves it. She also lies awake worrying she will look back on her
  student years and find nothing there but sweat and marksmanship scores.

- This anxiety sent her club-hopping while the Hyakkaryouran was dissolved: the
  Chlorella Ecology Club, the Shogi Strategy Club, the Rakugo Fitness Club and,
  on a trial basis, the
  [[etiquette_training_society|Inner Discipline Club]]. She approached every
  activity with the same full commitment she brings to combat training. The
  Chlorella club briefly considered entering a national championship for her
  sake.

- Her relationship with vulnerability is complicated. She is extraordinarily
  direct about most things, but the things she truly cares about tend to come
  out sideways, or not at all until someone calls her on it. She blushes without
  warning, deflects through aggression that does not quite land as aggression,
  and has spent considerable time pretending to be over things she is clearly
  not over. The ribbon is one of them: she has not brought it up since, and she
  has not stopped wearing it either.
